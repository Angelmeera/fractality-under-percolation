# Figures

The figures of the manuscript, regenerated by the scripts in `src/` from the files in
`results/`, each as a 600-dpi PNG and a vector EPS drawn at the printed width of
5.0 in (see `src/paper_style.py`). File names predate the paper's numbering; the paper
figure number is given below and in `docs/REPRODUCING.md`.

| file | paper | subject |
|---|---|---|
| `fig1_pc_dB` | Fig. 1 | susceptibility and `d_B(p)` through the percolation transition |
| `fig3_alpha_beta_corrected` | Fig. 2 | `α(p)`, `β(p)` and the mass-law reconstruction under **three** box-mass conventions |
| `fig4_clustering` | Fig. 3 | clustered `G^A` versus triangle-free `G^B` |
| `fig9_fss` | Fig. 4 | finite-size scaling for the percolation critical exponents |
| `fig8_grid_and_size` | Fig. 5 | box-size grid selection, and the parity effect on the WWW |
| `fig6_dblp` | Fig. 6 | percolation of the DBLP `w ≥ 60` backbone: `χ(p)`, then `d_B`, γ, δ, α, β of the largest component |
| `fig7_fractality` | Fig. 7 | the fractality test on fractal networks and controls |
| `fig5_convergence` | — | closure residual and simulated `p_c` against system size (not in the current manuscript; its numbers are in Table 3 and Sec. 4.2) |

`fig3_alpha_beta_corrected` (Fig. 2) is the one to look at first. The three conventions
differ only in what counts as the mass of a box that percolation has fragmented, and
they disagree about *which* exponent is the fragile one — which is why the paper draws
no conclusion on that point.
